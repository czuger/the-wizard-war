# La guerre des magiciens — Rules

*The Wizard War*, by Dominique Tellier and Michel Brassinne, published in *Jeux & Stratégie* no. 46 (August–September 1987), pages 64 and 73–74 (rule insert, "Encart").
These rules are translated from the scans in `work/` (`Jeux & Stratégie 046 - Page 066/075/076`). French terms from the original are shown in *italics* the first time they appear.

## Theme

The two wizards Xéphis and Yaboul fight to rule the county of Arghtal. The players are alchemists who have come to the fortified city of Argh to profit from the war. They do not take sides. They buy laboratories, make magic items linked to the two cults (and to the cult-free abbot Toratsa), and sell them through peddlers and caravans. Meanwhile they must deal with their rivals and with the fanatics.

- Selling Xéphis grimoires or Yaboul pentacles is profitable but risky: you have to pick a side, or keep switching.
- Making Toratsa talismans is safer but earns less.

## Players and goal

- **2 to 6 players.**
- **Goal:** be the richest alchemist in the city when the game ends.
- **End of game:** the game ends after a time limit or a number of turns that the players agree on before starting (about one hour the first time, or 8 to 10 turns). The richest player wins.

## Components

- One 6-sided die.
- Banknotes (Monopoly-style, or home-made) in 100, 500, 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 *écus*.
- Two envelopes: one for **magic items**, one for the **collective expense**.
- **The board:** the fortified city of Argh, divided into districts (*quartiers*, six colours), and its surroundings split into three zones: the **outskirts** (*faubourgs*), the **countryside** (*campagne*) and the **mountain** (*montagne*). The surroundings contain 28 villages, 2 castles (marked by a banner) and 2 monasteries (marked by a large cross), joined by paths. The city has gates where pawns enter and leave.
- **Pawns:**
  - 21 alchemist **laboratories** (shown as a retort).
  - 24 **peddlers** (*colporteurs*), 4 in each of 6 colours, shown as a bundle.
  - 18 **caravans** (*caravaniers*), 3 in each of 6 colours, shown as a wagon.
  - 7 red **rebels** (*rebelles*) pawns, shown as an armed mob.
  - 42 **cult markers** (*marqueurs de culte*): 21 Xéphis (grimoire) and 21 Yaboul (pentacle). They show which cult a village, castle or monastery follows.
  - 80 **magic items** (*objets magiques*):
    - 20 Toratsa talismans (owl marked **T**)
    - 25 Xéphis grimoires (marked **X**)
    - 35 Yaboul pentacles (marked **Y**)
  - 24 **fanatics** (*fanatiques*): 14 of the Xéphis cult and 10 of the Yaboul cult. Each shows the cult's initial one to three times. The number of initials is the pawn's **strength** (1, 2 or 3).

## Setup

1. Choose how the game ends: by time or by number of turns.
2. One player acts as the **banker**.
3. Each player receives **5,000 écus** and **one laboratory**.
4. Shuffle the fanatic pawns face down and deal them out:
   - 6 each with 2 players
   - 5 each with 3 or 4 players
   - 4 each with 5 or 6 players

   Each player looks at their fanatics and keeps them face down in front of them, off the board.
5. Put all the magic-item pawns in an envelope. The banker keeps it.
6. Choose the first player at random.
7. Each player chooses a district of the city (one of the six colours) and places their laboratory there.

## Turn structure

A turn has **seven phases**. In each phase, the players take that phase's actions one after another, going clockwise (to the left). When everyone has played a phase, the next phase starts. After the seven phases, a new turn begins.

To balance the game, the first player of each new turn is the player to the **left** of the previous turn's first player.

| # | Phase |
|---|-------|
| I | Announcements and expenses (*Annonces et dépenses*) |
| II | Conversions and rebel movement (*Conversions et déplacements des rebelles*) |
| III | Making magic items (*Fabrication d'objets magiques*) |
| IV | Fanatic attacks (*Attaques de fanatiques*) |
| V | Peddler and caravan movement, sales (*Déplacements des colporteurs et des caravaniers, vente*) |
| VI | Collective expense result and sales in the city (*Résultat des dépenses et ventes intra-muros*) |
| VII | Purchases and negotiations (*Achats et négociations*) |

---

### Phase I — Announcements and expenses

**Principle:** each alchemist makes choices about making magic items. They can safely make Toratsa talismans, or take the more dangerous but more profitable route of making Xéphis and Yaboul items.

#### I.1 Announcements (*Annonces*)

- **Action:** each player may place **one** of their fanatic pawns face down in the middle of the city, in the great hall. A player may place at most one fanatic per turn.
- **Explanation:** the fanatics act in phase IV. The dominant group of fanatics raids the laboratories to destroy magic items that do not belong to its cult.

#### I.2 Expenses (*Dépenses*)

- **Action:** the banker passes an envelope around. Each player secretly puts in as much money as they want, or nothing. The banker then closes the envelope and keeps it.
- **Explanation:** in phase VI, the total collected decides how many magic items can be sold in the city.

---

### Phase II — Conversions and rebel movement

**Principle:** each village, castle and monastery is either **neutral** (no cult) or ruled by **Xéphis** or **Yaboul**. A conversion means placing a cult marker face up with one cult's emblem, or changing a marker that is already there.

#### II.1 Conversions

- **Action:** in turn, each player takes **exactly one** of these three actions:
  - place a cult marker on a village, castle or monastery that has none;
  - flip over a cult marker;
  - replace a **face-down** cult marker with a marker of the **opposite** cult, face up. The player then places a **rebels** pawn next to it.
- **Restrictions:**
  - a) **First turn:** a cult marker may only go on a village that a path connects to an edge of the board.
  - b) **Second turn and later:** a cult marker may only go on a village that a path connects to another village that already has a cult marker (face up or face down).
  - c) **Castles and monasteries never change cult.** Once a marker is on one of them, it is never changed.

#### II.2 Rebel movement and fights

- **Action:** the **first player of the turn** moves **all** the rebels pawns on the board. They then resolve the resulting fights, in the order they choose, following the rules below.
- A rebels pawn moves **one village** per turn along a path.
- **Fights:** when a rebels pawn arrives in a village that holds one or more peddlers or caravans, there is a fight, even if those pawns belong to the player moving the rebels. That player attacks **one** pawn of their choice and rolls the die:

  | Die | Result |
  |-----|--------|
  | 1–2 | The brawl calms down. The rebels pawn is removed from the game. |
  | 3–4 | The attacked pawn loses the magic item it was carrying (returned to the envelope). If it carried nothing, it must stop for one turn (turn the pawn over). |
  | 5–6 | The rebels win. The victim, and anything it carried, is eliminated and removed from the board. The banker removes the rebels pawn from the game. |

  Whatever the result, the rebels pawn is removed from the game after the fight.

---

### Phase III — Making magic items

**Principle:** keep apart the magic items a player has **already made** (their **stock**) and the items **being made** this turn.

Each player places the items being made face down at the edge of the board. Their stock, also face down, stays closer to them.

- **Action:** the banker passes the magic-item envelope to the first player of the turn. In turn, each player **secretly** takes one or more magic items (Xéphis grimoire, Yaboul pentacle or Toratsa talisman) and places them face down in front of them, against the edge of the board.
- Each player takes **as many items as they have laboratories**.

---

### Phase IV — Fanatics

- **Action:** if nobody placed a fanatic in phase I.1, skip to the next phase.
- Otherwise, turn over the fanatic pawn(s) and work out the **Dominant Fanatic** (*la Dominante Fanatique*, "DF"): add up the points of each cult (count the initials). The cult with more points is the DF.
- **Tie:** the fanatics do not raid the laboratories. Go to the next phase.
- Otherwise, each player turns over the magic items they made in **this turn's** making phase. Earlier items, which form their stock, are not affected. **Toratsa talismans are never counted here.** There are three cases:
  1. **All** the items are of the DF's cult. The fanatics help the alchemist, who receives **one free magic item of that cult** (the banker takes it from the envelope and gives it to the player).
  2. The items are of **both** cults. The fanatics burn the items of the cult opposed to the DF. They are removed from the game.
  3. **All** the items are of the cult opposed to the DF. The fanatics destroy them, together with **all the items in the alchemist's personal stock**, without exception.

Magic items made this turn that survive go into the player's stock. From then on, peddlers and caravans can carry them.

---

### Phase V — Peddler and caravan movement, sales

**Principle:** peddlers are lone sellers and slow. Caravans have wagons and are faster. Both carry magic items out of the city and sell them.

#### V.1 Movement

- Each player makes all their moves, then sells their magic items.
- A player may not own more than **4 peddlers** and **3 caravans**.
- A peddler or caravan carries **only one item** at a time. The item is placed face down and covered by the pawn carrying it.
- A peddler moves **one village** per turn. A caravan moves **one or two villages** per turn, as the player chooses. Both follow paths.
- **Leaving the city:** the player places the peddler or caravan on the gate of their choice and moves it from there. **Coming back:** the pawn is removed as soon as it reaches one of the city gates.

#### V.2 Selling

- A peddler or caravan may only sell its item in a village **of the same cult as the item**, and only if **no other item has been sold there this turn** (one item per village per turn). Otherwise, or if the village is neutral, nothing can be sold there.
- The sold item goes back to the bank, and the alchemist immediately receives the amount shown in **Table 1**.
- The unloaded peddler or caravan must then go back to its laboratory at the same speed as on the way out.
- Outside the city, **Toratsa talismans can only be sold in castles** (see Table 1).
- **Only monasteries** buy as many items as are offered to them in the same turn.
- Any number of peddlers and caravans may be in the same village. However, if one of them **ends its move** in a village occupied by rebels, there is a fight. The owner rolls the die against their own pawn (see the rebel fights in II.2). A caravan that passes through a village with rebels without stopping there is not affected.
- An item may be passed from one pawn to another if both pawns are in the same village at the end of their moves. If both pawns belong to the same player, the transfer happens immediately. Otherwise, the transfer and any payment for it happen in the negotiations phase (VII).

#### Table 1 — Magic item sale prices (écus)

| Item | City (*Ville*) | Outskirts (*Faubourgs*) | Countryside (*Campagne*) | Mountain (*Montagne*) |
|------|---------------:|------------------------:|-------------------------:|----------------------:|
| Toratsa talisman | 4,000 | — | 12,500 | — |
| Xéphis grimoire | 4,500 | 5,000 | 7,000 | 13,000 |
| Yaboul pentacle | 3,500 | 5,000 | 8,000 | 16,000 |

### Phase VI — Sales in the city

- The banker opens the envelope holding the écus the players put in during phase I.2. The total is announced to everyone.
- Look up **Table 2** at the crossing of the **total number of laboratories on the board** and the **collective expense**. The number shown is how many magic items **all the alchemists together** may sell in the city this turn.
- The first player of the turn says whether they want to sell one item from their stock at the "City" price in Table 1. If so, they sell it immediately. The player to their left then decides, and so on around the table, until the number of items from Table 2 is reached.

#### Table 2 — Number of magic items that can be sold in the city

| Laboratories \ Collective expense | under 700 | 700–1,999 | 2,000–3,999 | 4,000–5,999 | 6,000–7,999 | 8,000+ |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2 to 4 | 0 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 5 to 9 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| 10 to 14 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 9 | 9 |
| 15 to 21 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 9 | 12 |

---

### Phase VII — Purchases and negotiations

#### Purchases

These are paid to the bank:

| Item | Price |
|------|------:|
| Peddler | 3,000 écus |
| Caravan | 7,000 écus |
| Laboratory | 11,000 écus |

Peddlers and caravans that are in the city may be **sold back to the bank at half price**. The bank is **closed** for this during the **last two turns** of the game, or the last quarter of an hour in a timed game.

#### Negotiations

The players may hold a short round of negotiations, in the open or in secret, to form alliances. For example, they may agree on which fanatics to place in the first phase of the next turn.

## Notes on unclear points in the original

- **Collective expense money:** the rules do not say what happens to the écus in the expense envelope after phase VI. The text says only that "the sum is made public". The obvious reading is that the money is spent and goes to the bank.
- **"Flip over a cult marker" (II.1):** markers are placed face up, but restriction (b) and the third action mention face-down markers. Flipping a marker therefore seems to switch it between face up and face down. The rules do not say what a face-down marker means for selling.
- **Laboratory districts:** each player puts their first laboratory in a district they choose (one of the six colours). The rules do not say where laboratories bought later go, or where an unloaded peddler or caravan "returns to its laboratory" when a player has several.
- **Stopped pawns:** a pawn turned over after a 3–4 fight result "stops for one turn". The rules do not say when it is turned back.
- **Table 2 with only 1 laboratory:** the table starts at 2 laboratories. In a 2-player game both players start with one laboratory each, which gives 2 in total, so the first row applies.
- **Fanatic strengths:** the rules give 14 Xéphis and 10 Yaboul fanatics, each with strength 1 to 3, but not how many of each strength. This implementation assumes Xéphis 4×1, 5×2, 5×3 and Yaboul 4×1, 3×2, 3×3 (`src/domain/fanatic_pool.rs`).
- **Toratsa talismans in a ravaged laboratory (IV, case 3):** the fanatics ignore Toratsa talismans when judging, so this implementation lets the talismans being made survive, while the whole stock is destroyed.
- **The map:** the original board has no names and no list of paths. `src/domain/board.rs` transcribes the 32 sites and their paths from the board picture, with invented French names. Only four villages have a path to the edge of the board (two in the north, two in the west), so on the first turn the last players can only flip markers.
- **Conversion restrictions:** restrictions (a) and (b) are applied to placing a new marker, on any site (a castle or a monastery is joined to the map like a village); flipping or replacing is not restricted. The cult markers are limited to 21 per cult and the rebels to the 7 pawns of the box.
- **Rebel movement:** a rebels pawn must move to an adjacent **village**; castles and monasteries stop it, and it never leaves the board or enters the city. With no peddlers or caravans yet, no fight happens.
